# Offer and listing copy

Selling copy is not decoration on top of a product. It is the product, explained so clearly that
wanting it becomes obvious. Clarity outsells cleverness every time.

## Start by finding the real value
Before writing, answer:
- **What does this actually do for the person?** Not the feature — the outcome in their life.
- **Who is it for, specifically?** Copy for everyone persuades no one.
- **What is the moment they need it?** Write to that moment.
- **What would make them hesitate?** Answer that in the copy, don't hope they forget.

## The shape of a listing that sells
1. **Name + one line** — what it is, plainly. If a stranger can't tell what they're buying from the
   first line, nothing else matters.
2. **The outcome** — one or two sentences on what changes for them.
3. **The specifics** — size, materials, timing, what's included, what's not. Concrete detail builds
   trust; vagueness reads as hiding something.
4. **Why this one** — what makes it different, honestly stated.
5. **What to do next** — the action, plainly.

## Voice rules
- **Write like the leader in the category**, not like a beginner asking permission to be considered.
  Confident, warm, specific. Never "we're just a small business trying our best" — that invites doubt.
- **Concrete beats adjective.** "Ships in two days, fits a 15-inch laptop" beats "amazing quality
  and super convenient." Adjectives are claims; specifics are evidence.
- **Short sentences carry weight.** Cut every word that isn't working.
- **Their words, not industry words.** Say what a customer would say out loud to a friend.
- **One idea per paragraph.** Walls of text are skipped, not read.

## Never
- **Never invent proof.** No made-up reviews, no fabricated statistics, no "thousands of happy
  customers" if that isn't true, no borrowed logos. It is dishonest, and it collapses trust the moment
  it's questioned. If proof is thin, sell on specifics instead — those are always real.
- **Never promise outcomes you cannot control** — especially anything health, income, or results
  related. Describe what the thing IS and what it DOES, not what it will guarantee.
- **No hype punctuation.** Exclamation marks and ALL CAPS read as desperate, not excited.
- **Don't hide the price** if the page's job is to sell. Hidden price reads as expensive-and-evasive.

## Length, by surface
- **Product listing:** short and scannable, specifics as a list.
- **Service page:** longer — the objection handling IS the selling.
- **Email:** one idea, one action. If it needs two, it's two emails.
- **Social post:** the hook is the first line; the rest only gets read if the first line earns it.

## Before you show her
- Read it out loud. Anything you'd never say to a person, rewrite.
- Check every factual claim in it is one she can stand behind.
- Give her ONE version you believe in, plus an alternative for anything that's genuinely a taste
  call — not five options for her to sort through. Ranking is your job, not hers.
- If the copy needs a fact you don't have (price, timing, materials), ask for that one fact rather
  than inventing a plausible-sounding placeholder that could ship by accident.
