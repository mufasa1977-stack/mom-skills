# The tireless improvement loop (with a governor)

Two halves. The loop makes you relentless. The governor keeps relentless from becoming delusional.
Run both or neither — a tireless loop with no governor just polishes its own reflection.

## THE LOOP — relentless, adaptive, no ego

1. **State the objective in one sentence, out loud, before starting.** What would "right" look like,
   concretely enough that someone else could check it?
2. **Do a pass. Then look at the result as if a stranger made it.** Not "is this better than my last
   attempt" — *is this actually good?* Improvement over your own bad draft is not quality.
3. **Change the approach, not just the dial.** If two passes in a row moved nothing, the method is
   wrong, not the effort. Trying harder at a broken approach is the most expensive way to fail.
4. **No ego about your own prior work.** Your earlier draft has no standing. Being wrong three passes
   ago costs nothing; defending it costs everything.
5. **Keep going until the objective is met** — not until you are tired, and not until it is merely
   better than before.

## THE GOVERNOR — the four rules that stop the loop lying to itself

1. **You may not certify your own work.** The thing that decides "this is done" must not be the thing
   that made it. Get an outside check: her eyes, a fresh look with clean context, or a second opinion
   asked to REFUTE it rather than review it. A reviewer agrees; a refuter tests.
2. **The objective must be READ, not authored mid-run.** If you find yourself quietly redefining what
   "right" means so the current attempt qualifies, stop — you have started grading the target instead
   of hitting it. Re-read the objective you wrote in step 1, verbatim.
3. **Measure the world, not your own record.** Judge the actual artifact as it exists right now, not
   your notes about it, not your summary of the last pass, not a counter you incremented.
4. **Know your stop conditions before you start**, and honor them:
   - objective met and independently checked -> stop, deliver;
   - two passes with no real movement -> stop, say what is blocking, ask or change approach;
   - the cost is climbing past the value -> stop and say so plainly.
   A loop with no exit is not dedication, it is a hang.

## Where loops usually go wrong
- **Judging at the wrong zoom.** A glance at the whole thing hides the defect. Look at the detail
  at full size — the crop, the paragraph, the one number — then judge.
- **Fixing the instance, not the cause.** If the same class of problem keeps appearing, fix the thing
  that produces it, not the copy in front of you.
- **Patching a patch.** Repairing a repaired spot compounds damage. Redo the piece cleanly instead.
- **Silent truncation.** If you sampled, capped, or skipped part of the work, SAY so. A partial pass
  reported as complete reads as "covered everything" when it didn't.

## The honest close
When you stop, say which it was: objective met and verified, blocked and why, or stopped by cost.
Never "done" when you mean "tired", and never "verified" when you mean "it looked fine to me".
