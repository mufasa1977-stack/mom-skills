# Business page build

The job is not "a page exists." The job is: a stranger lands on it, understands the offer in five
seconds, believes it, and takes the one action you want. Build to that.

## Before you write a line of code, answer three questions
1. **Who is landing here, and what do they want?** Not what the business wants to say — what the
   visitor came to find out.
2. **What is the ONE action?** Book, buy, call, sign up. One primary action per page. A page with
   four equal buttons has no call to action.
3. **Why should they believe it?** Real proof: actual photos, real names, real numbers, real work.

## Structure that works (top to bottom)
1. **Hero** — a plain-language headline saying what this is and who it's for, a one-line subhead,
   the primary button, and one strong real image. No jargon, no "welcome to our website."
2. **The offer** — what they get, in their words, as scannable items. Not a wall of paragraphs.
3. **Proof** — testimonials with real names, photos of real work, credentials, numbers that are true.
4. **Objection handling** — price, timeline, "will this work for me", how to get started.
5. **Close** — restate the offer, repeat the same primary button. Someone who scrolled to the bottom
   is interested; do not make them scroll back up.

## The craft rules that separate professional from homemade
- **Real imagery, never drawn-in-code shapes as the hero.** A real photograph of the real thing beats
  a gradient blob or a clip-art icon every time. If there is no real photo, that is a job to solve,
  not a thing to fake with graphics.
- **Hierarchy is a size and weight decision.** One clearly biggest thing per screen. If everything is
  bold, nothing is.
- **Generous whitespace.** Cramped equals cheap. Give elements room to breathe.
- **Contrast is non-negotiable.** Body text must be easily readable against its background — dark text
  on light, or light on dark, with real separation. Grey-on-grey looks "designed" and reads terribly,
  especially on a phone in daylight. Depth and softness must NEVER cost you readability.
- **Two fonts maximum**, consistent sizes. Pick a scale and stick to it.
- **Buttons look like buttons** — solid, labelled with the action ("Book a call", not "Submit").
- **Confident voice, never beginner-framing.** Write like the category leader, not like someone
  apologizing for existing. Never invent proof, testimonials, or numbers that aren't real — false
  proof is both wrong and the fastest way to lose trust.

## Mobile is the real page
Most visitors are on a phone. Check the phone layout FIRST, not last. Tap targets big enough for a
thumb, text that doesn't need pinching, nothing running off the side of the screen, images that don't
push the layout wide. A page that only looks right on a laptop is a broken page.

## Before you call it done — actually look at it
1. Open it in a real browser and LOOK, don't just confirm the code ran.
2. Check it at phone width as well as full width.
3. Click every link and button; make sure each goes where it claims.
4. Read the text out loud once — typos and awkward lines surface instantly.
5. Check it in both light and dark appearance if the design responds to that.
6. Zoom in on the details — small text, edges, spacing. Defects hide at a glance and show up close.
Only then tell her it's ready, and say what you checked.

## Never do these
- Publish anything live without her explicit yes — publishing is her decision, every time.
- Put a real person's photo on a page without permission.
- Fake reviews, fake logos, fake "as seen in", or invented statistics.
- Collect passwords or card details on a hand-built form; use a real payment/booking provider.
